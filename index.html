01 — SURFACE

Why our seas matter

The Philippines is an archipelagic country whose communities, food systems, biodiversity, and coastal economies are closely connected to the sea.

◉

Marine biodiversity

Philippine waters support diverse marine ecosystems, including coral reefs, mangroves, seagrass, fish, turtles, birds, and other wildlife.

⌁

Food & fisheries

Healthy coastal waters support fisheries and provide food and livelihoods for communities that depend on marine resources.

⌂

Coastal communities

Pollution can reach beyond the water itself, affecting communities, tourism, livelihoods, and the places people call home.

✦

Our well-being

Healthy marine environments contribute to ecological, social, and economic well-being. Protecting them is a shared responsibility.

From sachets to seas

Plastic pollution is linked to everyday consumption, single-use packaging, gaps in waste collection, and the movement of discarded materials through waterways. Earth.Org notes that plastic often reaches the sea through rivers rather than entering the ocean directly.

Why rivers matter

Climate Impact Tracker describes urban runoff, improper disposal, inadequate collection, and flooding as pathways that can move plastic toward rivers and eventually the ocean. This makes marine pollution partly a land-management problem too.

04 — BELOW THE SURFACE

Manila Bay & oil pollution

In July 2024, several maritime incidents around Manila Bay led to oil contamination concerns affecting waters and nearby communities.

01
What happened?

Inquirer reported that MT Terra Nova, carrying 1.4 million liters of industrial fuel oil, sank off Bataan on July 25, 2024. Other vessel incidents followed.

02
Environmental concern

Oil can threaten habitats such as mangroves, coral reefs, and seagrass beds, which support marine species.

03
Fisheries & communities

Fishing restrictions and contamination concerns can interrupt income and access to seafood in affected areas.

04
Public-health precautions

Authorities advised avoiding contaminated waters and seafood from affected areas unless cleared by appropriate authorities.

07 — HUMAN TIDE

It reaches people, too.

Marine pollution can become a social and economic issue when ecosystems, food sources, livelihoods, and coastal activities are disrupted.

₱

Livelihoods

Fishing disruptions can mean lost income for fisherfolk and related businesses.

◒

Food security

Pollution incidents can create concerns about the safety and availability of seafood from affected areas.

⌂

Coastal communities

Communities near polluted waters can face cleanup, environmental, and access challenges.

✦

Tourism

Polluted beaches and damaged marine environments can reduce the appeal and value of coastal destinations.
